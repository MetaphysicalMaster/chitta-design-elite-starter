# The Reactivation Audit + Live Demo (sales playbook)

INTERNAL. The discovery → proof → close sequence. MM Bible shape:
**door → wedge → proof → expand → cascade.** Lead the conversation with the
finished site (door), demo the missed-call (wedge), prove with their own
reactivation $ (proof), expand to the department, ride the group referral (cascade).

---

## 1. Discovery questions (get the inputs for the math)
- Roughly how many patient records are in your system? When did you last reach out to lapsed patients?
- Average ticket for a tox visit? A filler/laser visit? Typical patient's annual spend?
- How many inbound calls a day, roughly? Who answers when your injectors are in a room?
- How many Google reviews do you have, and when was the last one?
- What's your rebook/return rate look like — do you know who's overdue?

## 2. The leak math (turn answers into dollars on a one-page audit)
**Missed-call leak / month**
`calls_per_month × 0.62 missed × close_rate(≈0.25) × avg_ticket`
e.g. 300 calls × 0.62 × 0.25 × $600 = **~$27,900/mo** walking to competitors.

**Dormant-patient leak (one-time recovery)**
`records × due_share(≈0.15–0.30) × reactivation_conv(≈0.05–0.10) × avg_ticket`
e.g. 4,000 × 0.20 × 0.07 × $600 = **~$33,600** sitting in the list right now.

**Recurring reactivation (the clock, monthly)**
`active_tox_patients ÷ ~3.5-mo cadence × reactivation_lift × avg_ticket` — frame as a monthly metronome, not a one-off.

**Review gap**
Their review count + recency vs the #1 map-pack competitor in their metro. The *gap* is the pitch (Google weights volume, recency, response rate).

> Present these as **ranges grounded in industry research**, labeled as such — never as a guaranteed outcome. The audit shows the *size of the leak*, not a promise.

## 3. The live missed-call demo (the close)
On the call, with their permission:
1. "Call your own front desk number right now and let it ring out."
2. It rings out (it always does — that's the point; let the silence land).
3. Our system texts them back in <30s, in a draft of their clinic's voice, and offers to book.
4. Let them feel the derailment — *"wait, it already texted them?"* That single moment closes harder than any deck (MM Bible Law II: stop the train of thought with a true demonstration).

## 4. Objection handling
- **"It's expensive."** → One filled chair pays for the month. The audit already showed $20k+/mo leaking; the retainer recovers a fraction of that in week one.
- **"We have a front desk."** → And they're excellent — at the chair. This is for the calls they *can't* reach mid-procedure and the patients no one has time to chase. It makes your team look superhuman, not redundant.
- **"Is this spam / is it legal?"** → We only ever contact patients with documented consent, every message has instant opt-out, we sign a BAA, and messages never mention treatments or health details. Compliance is built into onboarding, not bolted on.
- **"We tried an agency and it didn't work."** → They sold you ads into a leaking bucket. We plug the free leaks *first* — your own list, your own calls, your own reviews — and only run ads once the chairs are already filling.
- **"Will an AI sound like a robot to my patients?"** → Every word is written in *your injector's* voice, not a generic bot. That's the whole point — and the part nobody else builds.

## 5. The close sequence
1. **Door:** "Here's the site we built you (and 6 peer clinics) — live." 
2. **Wedge:** the live missed-call demo on their own line.
3. **Proof:** the Reactivation Audit numbers from *their* list.
4. **Offer:** "Start with Recover. One filled chair pays for it. 14-day proof."
5. **Founder ask (one clinic only):** founder rate for a named case study.
6. **Expand → Cascade:** once it works, the full Growth OS + a "refer a clinic" loop into multi-location groups, DSO/MSO chains, injector-trainer networks, and Allē/ASPIRE rep relationships — the medspa version of JP's franchise advisory-board cascade.
